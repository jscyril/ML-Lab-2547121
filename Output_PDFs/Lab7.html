Task 3A: Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation¶

A single train-test split can be unusually easy or difficult, especially with a small dataset. Stratified 5-fold cross-validation evaluates the model five times while preserving the class proportions in every fold. Each sample is used for validation once and for training four times.

In [7]:
cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)

cv_models = {
    "Unrestricted tree": DecisionTreeClassifier(random_state=42),
    "Depth-3 tree": DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=3, random_state=42)
}

fold_rows = []
summary_rows = []

for model_name, model in cv_models.items():
    scores = cross_validate(
        model,
        X,
        y,
        cv=cv,
        scoring="accuracy",
        return_train_score=True
    )

    for fold, (train_score, validation_score) in enumerate(
        zip(scores["train_score"], scores["test_score"]), start=1
    ):
        fold_rows.append({
            "Model": model_name,
            "Fold": fold,
            "Training Accuracy": train_score,
            "Validation Accuracy": validation_score
        })

    mean_train = scores["train_score"].mean()
    mean_validation = scores["test_score"].mean()
    summary_rows.append({
        "Model": model_name,
        "Mean Training Accuracy": mean_train,
        "Mean Validation Accuracy": mean_validation,
        "Validation Std. Dev.": scores["test_score"].std(ddof=1),
        "Train-Validation Gap": mean_train - mean_validation
    })

fold_results = pd.DataFrame(fold_rows)
cv_summary = pd.DataFrame(summary_rows)

print("Accuracy in each fold:")
display(
    fold_results.style.format({
        "Training Accuracy": "{:.2%}",
        "Validation Accuracy": "{:.2%}"
    })
)

print("Cross-validation summary:")
display(
    cv_summary.style.format({
        "Mean Training Accuracy": "{:.2%}",
        "Mean Validation Accuracy": "{:.2%}",
        "Validation Std. Dev.": "{:.2%}",
        "Train-Validation Gap": "{:.2%}"
    })
)

Cross-Validation Interpretation¶

  • The unrestricted tree obtains 100.00% mean training accuracy and 95.33% mean validation accuracy with a standard deviation of 3.80%. Its train-validation gap is 4.67 percentage points.
  • The depth-3 tree obtains 97.50% mean training accuracy and 96.00% mean validation accuracy with a standard deviation of 2.79%. Its gap is only 1.50 percentage points.

The original 100% holdout accuracy was therefore a favorable result rather than a guarantee of perfect performance on unseen data. The unrestricted tree shows mild overfitting because it memorizes every training fold but performs less accurately during validation. The depth-3 model has slightly higher and more stable validation performance with a much smaller gap, strengthening its recommendation.

Increasing min_samples_split requires more samples before a node may be divided. This blocks splits in small nodes and therefore generally reduces depth and the number of leaves. In this experiment, increasing it from 2 to 5 reduces depth from 5 to 3 and leaves from 8 to 5, while testing accuracy improves from 93.33% to 96.67%. Values 5, 10, and 20 happen to produce the same tree structure.

A larger min_samples_leaf forces every terminal node to represent more training samples. This prevents tiny leaves that may memorize isolated or noisy records, reducing variance and overfitting risk. If set too high, it can oversmooth real class boundaries and cause underfitting. Here, raising the value from 1 to 5 reduces depth from 5 to 3 and leaves from 8 to 5, while testing accuracy remains 93.33%. Values 5 and 10 produce the same structure on this split.

Task 9: Final Questions and Recommendations¶

1. What is the role of the criterion parameter?¶

criterion defines how the quality of a candidate split is measured. Gini impurity and entropy both measure class mixing; the algorithm chooses the split with the greatest weighted impurity reduction. Gini uses squared class probabilities, while entropy uses their logarithms.

2. How does max_depth influence underfitting and overfitting?¶

A very small max_depth limits the rules so strongly that the tree may underfit, as demonstrated by depth 1. A large or unlimited depth increases flexibility and can fit fine details or noise, increasing overfitting risk. An intermediate depth can retain the useful class boundaries while limiting variance.

3. Why do larger min_samples_split and min_samples_leaf values produce simpler trees?¶

min_samples_split prevents nodes with too few records from splitting, while min_samples_leaf prevents splits that would create very small children. Both rules stop growth in the lower parts of the tree, resulting in fewer splits, shallower paths, and fewer leaves.

4. Which hyperparameter had the greatest impact?¶

max_depth had the greatest observed impact on performance. Across its tested range, testing accuracy changed from 66.67% to 96.67%, training accuracy changed from 66.67% to 100%, and actual depth changed from 1 to 5. In comparison, changing min_samples_split changed testing accuracy only from 93.33% to 96.67%, while min_samples_leaf left testing accuracy at 93.33% for every tested value. Changing Gini to entropy made no difference to the reported metrics.

5. Recommended model¶

The most suitable model from these experiments is:

DecisionTreeClassifier(criterion="gini", max_depth=3, random_state=42)

It is recommended because it achieves the highest stratified-holdout testing accuracy, 96.67%, with 98.33% training accuracy, depth 3, and only 5 leaves. Its 5-fold cross-validation accuracy is also 96.00%, with a smaller train-validation gap than the unrestricted tree. It therefore performs better and is substantially simpler than the default depth-5, 8-leaf tree. Gini and entropy perform identically here, so Gini is retained as the standard and computationally simpler default.
