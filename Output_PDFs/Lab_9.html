  • Standardization is fitted only on training folds through a pipeline.

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    X_cancer, y_cancer, test_size=0.20, stratify=y_cancer, random_state=RANDOM_STATE
)
print(f"Training samples: {len(X_train)} | Testing samples: {len(X_test)}")

# Brief kernel comparison on training data only. The required final model remains linear.
kernel_results = []
for kernel in ["linear", "rbf", "poly", "sigmoid"]:
    candidate = Pipeline([
        ("scaler", StandardScaler()),
        ("svm", SVC(kernel=kernel, C=1)),
    ])
    scores = cross_val_score(candidate, X_train, y_train, cv=5, scoring="f1_macro")
    kernel_results.append({"Kernel": kernel, "Mean CV Macro F1": scores.mean(), "CV Std. Dev.": scores.std()})
kernel_comparison = pd.DataFrame(kernel_results).sort_values("Mean CV Macro F1", ascending=False)
display(kernel_comparison.style.format({"Mean CV Macro F1": "{:.4f}", "CV Std. Dev.": "{:.4f}"}))

svm_pipeline = Pipeline([
    ("scaler", StandardScaler()),
    ("svm", SVC(kernel="linear"))
])

param_grid = {
    "svm__C": [0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100],
    "svm__class_weight": [None, "balanced"],
}
grid_search = GridSearchCV(
    svm_pipeline, param_grid, cv=5, scoring="f1_macro", n_jobs=-1, return_train_score=True
)
grid_search.fit(X_train, y_train)

cv_results = pd.DataFrame(grid_search.cv_results_)[
    ["param_svm__C", "param_svm__class_weight", "mean_train_score", "mean_test_score", "rank_test_score"]
].sort_values("rank_test_score")
display(cv_results.head(10).style.format({"mean_train_score": "{:.4f}", "mean_test_score": "{:.4f}"}))
print("Best parameters:", grid_search.best_params_)
print(f"Best mean cross-validation macro F1: {grid_search.best_score_:.4f}")

Explained variance and the 95% threshold¶

pca_all = PCA().fit(X_wine_scaled)
explained = pca_all.explained_variance_ratio_
cumulative = np.cumsum(explained)
min_components_95 = int(np.argmax(cumulative >= 0.95) + 1)

variance_table = pd.DataFrame({
    "Principal Component": [f"PC{i}" for i in range(1, len(explained) + 1)],
    "Explained Variance Ratio": explained,
    "Cumulative Variance": cumulative,
})
display(variance_table.style.format({
    "Explained Variance Ratio": "{:.4f}", "Cumulative Variance": "{:.4f}"
}))

PCA interpretation¶

  • PC1 captures the largest overall variation and is strongly influenced by phenol- and flavonoid-related measurements.
  • PC2 captures the next independent direction and is strongly influenced by alcohol and color-related measurements.
  • Nearby points have similar standardized chemical profiles.
  • Sign flips do not change a component's meaning; loading magnitude shows importance.

Advantages, limitations, and applications¶

Advantages

  • Reduces feature count, noise, storage, and later computation.
  • Removes multicollinearity because components are uncorrelated.
  • Makes high-dimensional data easier to visualize.

Limitations

  • Components are less interpretable than original features.
  • PCA is linear and can miss nonlinear structure.
  • It ignores class labels and is sensitive to feature scaling and outliers.

Applications

  • Visualization, compression, noise reduction, preprocessing, and feature extraction.

Extra Credit: Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)¶

LDA uses the known class labels and searches for directions that separate the classes. With three classes, it can produce at most two discriminants.
